## What to read

The document is a brief: a short update assembled from sources for one reader, who will act on it without opening the sources.

- `{INPUT_PATH}` — the brief as the reader receives it, with the title, series and date it was filed under.

You see the brief and the repository, not the mail, feeds, pages or tools it was assembled from. Every finding about a source is a question for a human to settle against that source.

## Attack it on these axes, in priority order

**1. The action it drives.** What will the reader do after reading it? Find the item that drives that action, and attack it first. A brief with one actionable item gets one finding there, not ten about the rest.

**2. Dates and staleness.** Is every time-bound statement dated, and is the date plausible for when the brief was filed? An undated "today", "this week" or "pending" is undated regardless of how confident it reads.

**3. Attribution.** Does each claim say where it came from? A claim with no source is an assertion by the brief itself. Flag the ones a reader would act on.

**4. Numbers.** Check the unit, the period and the base, not the digits. A change without its baseline has not been checked.

**5. The omission.** What would a reader of this series expect here that is missing: an item that was open last time, a failure that should have been reported, a section that is empty without saying so?

**6. Unverified passed off as verified.** A summary that reads as settled when its source is a rumour, a draft or a single message. A reader assumes the brief checked what it states.

## Ending

End with one line: can the reader act on this brief as written, YES or NO. If NO, name the single hypothesis that breaks it.
