## What to read

A markdown research brief, not code. Read the document the reader will actually receive — the rendered page or the markdown source, not the notes behind it.

## Attack it on these axes, in priority order

**1. Load-bearing claims.** A claim is load-bearing if removing it changes the conclusion; anything else is context. List them, then attack only those. A brief with three load-bearing claims and forty context sentences gets three findings, not forty.

**2. Citation strength.** Does the cited source *say* the claim, or is it merely consistent with it? Second-hand support — "the summary says the paper found" — is not support. Flag every claim resting on it.

**3. Numbers.** Check the unit, the date and the denominator, not the digits. A rate without its base has not been checked. A figure with no date is undated regardless of how precise it looks.

**4. The unstated assumption.** Find the load-bearing assumption the document never states. There is usually exactly one, and it is usually the thing the author found too obvious to write down.

**5. Does the conclusion follow from the page?** Read it as someone who has not seen the source material. Does the conclusion follow from what is actually on the page, or only from what a reader who already agrees would supply?

**6. Unverified passed off as verified.** Anything in the body that belongs in the Status section as explicitly unchecked. A reader assumes the body was checked; that assumption is the thing being protected.

## Ending

End with one line: does the central conclusion survive hostile reading, YES or NO. If NO, name the single finding that breaks it.
