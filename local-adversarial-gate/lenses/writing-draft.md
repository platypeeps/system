## What to read

- `{DRAFT_PATH}` — the `## Draft` section is the piece. `## Notes / angle` and `## Outline` are internal scaffolding that will never be published; read them only to understand what the author was trying to do.
- `{RESEARCH_PATH}` — the sourcing record. Every factual claim in the draft is supposed to trace back to something here.

## Attack it on these axes, in priority order

**1. Argument validity.** Does the evidence presented actually support the conclusion drawn? Find every logical jump. Where is the chain weakest?

**2. Citation strength.** For the load-bearing claims, does `research.md` actually support the sentence in the draft, or is the citation doing less work than the sentence claims? A source that establishes X being used to assert the stronger X+1 is the specific failure you are looking for. This axis exists because a prior verification pass on this repo produced a confident, wrong claim that reached a draft before a second pass caught it — assume that can happen again and that you are the second pass.

**3. The missing counterargument.** What is the single strongest objection a knowledgeable skeptic would raise that the piece never addresses? Check `research.md`'s own counterarguments section first and do not re-raise anything already handled there. Find one it missed.

**4. True but misleading.** Claims that are literally accurate yet leave a false impression, or framing that is defensible but would mislead a practitioner about what to actually do.

**5. Naivete and practicality.** Where would someone who has actually operated this at scale find the piece shallow? If it recommends an action, is that action workable in a real environment, or does the piece underestimate what it costs to do?

**6. Scope and stakes.** Does it overstate how much this matters? Is the harm real and material, or a small problem inflated to essay length?

## Ending

End with one line: does the central argument survive hostile reading, YES or NO. If NO, name the single finding that breaks it.
